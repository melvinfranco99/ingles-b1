Speaking

12–17 minutos · en pareja · 4 partes · 25 % de la nota. Hay dos examinadores: uno te hace las preguntas y otro escucha y evalúa. Te puntúan: Grammar & Vocabulary, Discourse Management (hablar con fluidez, ideas organizadas y relevantes), Pronunciation, Interactive Communication (responder, preguntar, negociar) y una nota global.

La regla de oro: responde + amplía (razón, ejemplo o detalle). "Do you prefer studying alone or with friends?" – "Alone, definitely. I get distracted easily, so I need silence. But before exams I sometimes meet a friend to test each other."

Parte 1 · Interview (2–3 min)

Preguntas personales: nombre, de dónde eres, estudios o trabajo, y luego temas cotidianos: What did you do last weekend? What do you enjoy doing with your friends? Tell us about a place you'd like to visit.

  • Responde con 2–3 frases. Usa tiempos variados (pasado, presente perfecto, futuro).
  • Prepara respuestas sobre los temas típicos: estudios/trabajo, tiempo libre, familia, comida, viajes, planes de futuro, tecnología.

Parte 2 · Describe a photo (1 minuto cada uno)

El examinador te da una foto y la describes tú solo durante un minuto. Luego tu compañero describe otra foto sobre un tema parecido.

Qué decir (en este orden)

  1. Visión general: This photo shows a family having a picnic in a park.
  2. Personas: quiénes son, qué llevan, qué hacen (present continuous): On the left, there's a man who is cutting some bread.
  3. Lugar y detalles: In the background I can see some trees and a lake.
  4. Suposiciones: It looks like a warm day. They seem to be enjoying themselves. Maybe it's somebody's birthday.
Si no sabes una palabra, descríbela: "a thing you use to…", "a kind of…". No te quedes callado: un minuto pasa rápido si sigues la lista.

Parte 3 · Collaborative task (2–3 min)

El examinador describe una situación y os da una hoja con 5–6 dibujos de ideas: "A young man is moving to a new city. He wants to make friends. Here are some things he could do. Talk together about the different things he could do, and say which would be best."

  • Comentad varias opciones (ventajas y desventajas) antes de decidir.
  • Interactúa: pregunta, reacciona, muestra acuerdo y desacuerdo con educación.
  • Al final, intentad decidir, pero no pasa nada si no estáis de acuerdo.
Ejemplo

A: Shall we start with joining a sports club? I think it's a great way to meet people.
B: I agree, especially if he likes football. But what if he isn't sporty?
A: Good point. Then maybe a language class would be better. Everyone there is new too.
B: That's true. What about volunteering? It might take a lot of time, though.
A: Hmm, I'm not sure. So, which do you think is best?
B: I'd say the language class. What do you think?
A: Yes, let's choose that.

Parte 4 · Discussion (3 min)

El examinador hace preguntas más generales relacionadas con la Parte 3: Is it easy to make friends in your town? Do you think people have fewer friends now because of social media?

  • Da tu opinión con una razón y un ejemplo personal.
  • Puedes comentar lo que dice tu compañero: "I agree with what Ana said, and also…"

Practica

Pulsa 🔊 para oír la pregunta, usa el temporizador y grábate. Luego escúchate: ¿has ampliado tus respuestas? ¿has usado conectores?

Parte 1

  • Where are you from?
  • Do you work or are you a student?
  • What do you enjoy doing in your free time?
  • Tell us about the last time you went on holiday.
  • What are you going to do next weekend?

Parte 2 · Describe esta foto (1 minuto)

Photo: Two teenagers are cooking in a small kitchen. The boy is cutting vegetables and the girl is reading a recipe on a tablet. There is flour on the table and they are laughing. Through the window you can see it is raining.

Parte 3 · Collaborative task

A school wants students to do more exercise. Here are some ideas. Talk together about the different ideas, and say which would be the best.

  • a longer lunch break with sports
  • a dance club
  • walking or cycling to school
  • a sports competition between classes
  • a new gym

Parte 4

  • Do young people do enough exercise nowadays?
  • Should sport be compulsory at school?
  • What do you do to stay healthy?
  • Is it better to do sport alone or in a team?
