Gramática para el B1 Preliminary

Esta es la gramática de nivel B1 que necesitas. Se evalúa directamente en la Parte 6 de Reading (una palabra por hueco) y en la Parte 5 (opciones), y es la base de una buena nota en Writing y Speaking (criterio Language / Grammar & Vocabulary). Cada tema tiene explicación en español, ejemplos y un ejercicio con corrección.

1. Present simple y present continuous

TiempoUsoEjemplo
Present simpleRutinas, hechos, horarios, verdades generalesShe works in a bank. The sun rises in the east.
Present continuousAcción ahora o en este periodo; situación temporal; cambiosI'm studying for an exam this month. Prices are going up.

Verbos de estado (no suelen ir en continuo): know, believe, understand, like, love, hate, want, need, prefer, belong, own, seem, mean, remember. ❌ I'm knowing → ✅ I know. Algunos cambian de significado: I think it's good (opinión) / I'm thinking about it (proceso); I have a car / I'm having lunch.

Adverbios de frecuencia: van antes del verbo principal y después de be: I often go… / She is always late.

2. Past simple, past continuous y used to

  • Past simple: acciones terminadas en un momento del pasado: I visited Rome in 2022.
  • Past continuous: acción en progreso en un momento del pasado o interrumpida: I was having a shower when the phone rang. Con while: While I was cooking, he was watching TV.
  • Used to + infinitivo: hábitos o estados del pasado que ya no ocurren: I used to live in Madrid. Did you use to play football? I didn't use to like fish. (También would para hábitos repetidos, no para estados.)

3. Present perfect simple y continuous

have/has + participio. Conecta el pasado con el presente:

  • Experiencias (sin decir cuándo): Have you ever been to London? I've never tried sushi.
  • Acciones recientes con resultado ahora: I've just finished. She's already left. Have you done it yet?
  • Situaciones que empezaron en el pasado y continúan: I've lived here for ten years / since 2015.

Continuous (have been + -ing): resalta la duración de una actividad: I've been waiting for an hour! It's been raining all day.

¡Ojo! Con un momento concreto del pasado (yesterday, last year, in 2020, ago) se usa past simple: ❌ I've seen him yesterday → ✅ I saw him yesterday. Y en español decimos «llevo 3 años aquí» → I've been here for 3 years (❌ I am here for 3 years).

4. Past perfect

had + participio: algo que ocurrió antes de otro momento del pasado: When I arrived, the film had already started. She was nervous because she had never flown before. Es muy útil en las historias del Writing.

5. El futuro

FormaUsoEjemplo
willDecisiones en el momento, predicciones (opinión), promesas, ofrecimientosI'll help you. I think it will rain.
going toPlanes e intenciones; predicción con evidenciaI'm going to study medicine. Look at those clouds – it's going to rain.
Present continuousPlanes concertados (con fecha, hora, entradas)I'm meeting Ana at six.
Present simpleHorarios fijosThe train leaves at 7.15.
Future continuousAcción en progreso en un momento futuroThis time tomorrow I'll be lying on the beach.

Después de when, as soon as, before, after, until, if se usa presente con significado futuro: I'll call you when I get home (❌ when I will get).

6. Modales de obligación, prohibición y consejo

ModalSignificadoEjemplo
mustObligación (la siente el hablante), normas escritasI must call my mum. Passengers must wear seat belts.
have toObligación externa; único que tiene pasado (had to)I have to wear a uniform. I had to wait.
mustn'tProhibiciónYou mustn't smoke here.
don't have to / don't need to / needn'tNo es necesarioYou don't have to come if you're busy.
should / ought toConsejoYou should see a doctor. You shouldn't eat so much sugar.
had betterConsejo fuerte (o algo malo pasará)You'd better hurry or you'll miss the train.
can / be allowed toPermisoYou can park here. We weren't allowed to use phones.

7. Modales de posibilidad y deducción

  • may / might / could = quizá: It might rain later. She may be at home.
  • must = estoy seguro de que sí: You've been working all day. You must be tired.
  • can't = estoy seguro de que no: That can't be Tom. He's in Paris. (❌ mustn't)
  • Habilidad: can / could / be able to: I could swim when I was five. I'll be able to help you tomorrow. I've never been able to dance.

8. Condicionales, unless y wish

TipoEstructuraUsoEjemplo
ZeroIf + presente, presenteVerdades generalesIf you heat ice, it melts.
FirstIf + presente, will + inf.Posible en el futuroIf it rains, we'll stay in.
SecondIf + pasado, would + inf.Imaginario o poco probableIf I had more money, I would travel. If I were you, I'd accept.

unless = if not: You won't pass unless you study. · I wish + pasado = me gustaría que fuera distinto ahora: I wish I lived near the beach.

9. La voz pasiva

be (en el tiempo correspondiente) + participio. Se usa cuando lo importante es la acción, no quién la hace. Quien la hace se introduce con by.

TiempoEjemplo
Present simpleEnglish is spoken all over the world.
Past simpleThe bridge was built in 1890.
Present perfectMy bike has been stolen!
Future / modalThe results will be sent by email. It can be done.
Present continuousThe road is being repaired.

10. Estilo indirecto (reported speech)

Al contar lo que dijo alguien, los tiempos suelen dar un paso atrás:

DirectoIndirecto
"I am tired."She said (that) she was tired.
"I went home." / "I have finished."He said he had gone home / he had finished.
"I will call you."She said she would call me.
"I can swim."He said he could swim.
"Where do you live?"She asked me where I lived. (sin do, orden de afirmación)
"Are you OK?"He asked me if / whether I was OK.
"Close the door." / "Don't run."She told me to close the door / not to run.

say / tell: She said (that)… / She told me (that)… (tell siempre lleva a quién). También cambian today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, here → there.

11. Oraciones de relativo

  • who (personas), which (cosas), that (personas o cosas, solo en especificativas), where (lugares), whose (cuyo), when (momentos).
  • Especificativas (sin comas): dan información necesaria. The girl who lives next door is a nurse. Si el relativo es objeto, se puede omitir: The book (that) I'm reading is great.
  • Explicativas (con comas): información extra. No se usa that ni se omite el relativo: My brother, who lives in Bilbao, is a chef.

12. Comparativos, superlativos, too / enough, so / such

  • bigger than, more interesting than, the best, the most expensive. Irregulares: good–better–best, bad–worse–worst, far–further–furthest.
  • as … as (igual): She's as tall as me. It isn't as cold as yesterday.
  • Intensificar: much / far / a lot bigger, a bit / slightly cheaper. Doble comparativo: the more you practise, the better you get.
  • too + adj. (demasiado): It's too expensive. · adj. + enough (suficiente): He isn't old enough. · enough + nombre: We haven't got enough time.
  • so + adjetivo / such (a) + adj. + nombre: It was so cold that… / It was such a cold day that…

13. Verbo + -ing o + to

+ -ing+ to + infinitivo
enjoy, mind, avoid, finish, suggest, imagine, practise, miss, keep, give up, can't stand, don't mind, look forward towant, decide, hope, plan, agree, refuse, promise, offer, manage, learn, afford, would like, seem, need
  • Después de preposición siempre -ing: I'm interested in learning. Thanks for helping. Before leaving…
  • like, love, hate, prefer, start, begin: con los dos casi sin diferencia.
  • remember / forget / stop / try cambian de significado: I stopped smoking (lo dejé) / I stopped to smoke (paré para fumar). Remember to lock the door (no lo olvides) / I remember locking it (recuerdo haberlo hecho).
  • make / let + objeto + infinitivo sin to: My parents let me go. She made me laugh.

14. Cuantificadores y artículos

  • Contables: many, a few (algunos), few (pocos), a number of · Incontables: much, a little (algo), little (poco), a great deal of · Ambos: a lot of / lots of, some, any, plenty of, no.
  • Incontables típicos en inglés: advice, information, news, furniture, luggage, homework, equipment, accommodation, weather, research (❌ an advice, informations → ✅ some advice, a piece of advice).
  • each / every + singular; all + plural; both (los dos), either (cualquiera de los dos), neither (ninguno de los dos), none of (ninguno de varios).
  • Artículos: sin the al hablar en general: Children love games. I like music. (❌ The children love…). Con the cosas concretas o únicas: the sun, the music in this café. Expresiones sin artículo: go to school/bed/work, at home, by bus.

16. Conectores

FunciónConectoresEjemplo
Contrastebut, although / even though + frase, however (al inicio, con coma), despite / in spite of + nombre o -ingAlthough it was cold, we swam. Despite the cold, we swam. It was cold. However, we swam.
Causabecause, as, since, because of + nombreAs it was late, we left. We stayed because of the rain.
Consecuenciaso, therefore, as a resultI was tired, so I went to bed.
Finalidadto, in order to, so thatI saved money to buy a car / so that I could buy a car.
Añadirand, also, too, as well, besides, what's more, in additionIt's cheap. What's more, it's near.
Ordenar (historias)first, then, after that, later, meanwhile, suddenly, in the end, eventually, finallySuddenly, the lights went out.

17. Question tags, preguntas indirectas y so / neither do I

  • Question tags (¿verdad?): afirmativa → tag negativo y al revés, con el mismo auxiliar: You're Spanish, aren't you? She didn't come, did she? You've finished, haven't you? Let's go, shall we?
  • Preguntas indirectas (más educadas): Could you tell me where the station is? Do you know what time it starts? I wonder if she's coming. (orden de afirmación, sin do).
  • Yo también / yo tampoco: I like jazz. – So do I. / I can't swim. – Neither can I. / I went. – So did I.

18. have something done y be / get used to

  • have / get + objeto + participio: alguien hace algo por ti: I had my hair cut. We're having the kitchen painted. I need to get my phone repaired.
  • be used to + -ing / nombre (estar acostumbrado): I'm used to getting up early. · get used to (acostumbrarse): You'll get used to the weather. ¡No confundir con used to + infinitivo (hábito pasado)!
