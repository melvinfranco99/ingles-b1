Writing (Partes 1 y 2)

45 minutos · 2 textos de unas 100 palabras. La Parte 1 (email) es obligatoria; en la Parte 2 eliges entre un artículo y una historia. Reparte el tiempo: 20 minutos por texto y 5 para revisar.

Parte 1 · Email (obligatorio)

Lees un email de un amigo, un profesor o un compañero, con 4 notas escritas a mano a los lados (p. ej. Great!, Tell Sam, Explain, Suggest…). Escribes la respuesta tratando las cuatro notas.

Estructura

  1. Saludo y frase de apertura: Hi Alex, Thanks for your email. It was great to hear from you!
  2. Un párrafo por nota (o dos notas por párrafo), desarrollando cada una con 1–2 frases: no basta con Yes.
  3. Cierre: Let me know what you think. Can't wait to see you! + Best wishes, / Love, + tu nombre.
Ejemplo de tarea
From: Jamie
Subject: Visit

Hi! I'm coming to your town for a weekend next month. [1 · Great!] I'd like to stay somewhere cheap – can you recommend anything? [2 · Yes, explain] What can we do on Saturday? [3 · Suggest…] And what's the best way to get there from the airport? [4 · Tell Jamie]

Respuesta modelo (≈ 110 palabras):

Hi Jamie,

Thanks for your email. I'm so happy you're coming to visit – it'll be great to see you again!

If you want somewhere cheap, I'd recommend the youth hostel near the old town. It's clean, friendly and only twenty euros a night. What's more, it's really close to my flat.

On Saturday, why don't we hire bikes and ride along the river to the beach? We could have lunch there and then go to a concert in the evening.

From the airport, the easiest way is to take the train. It leaves every fifteen minutes and takes half an hour. I'll meet you at the station.

Can't wait!
Lucía
Antes de escribir, numera las 4 notas en el enunciado y, al terminar, comprueba que cada número está en tu email.
✍ Practica: tu amiga Emma te escribe: "My class is organising a trip to your city. [Great!] We want to visit a museum – which one? [Tell Emma] We also need a place to eat lunch. [Suggest…] Can you meet us? [Yes, say when]"

Parte 2 · Artículo

Ves un anuncio en una revista o web en inglés: "Articles wanted! Tell us about your favourite place to relax. What do you do there? Why do you like it? The best articles will be published." Debes responder a todas las preguntas y dar tu opinión.

Estructura

  1. Título atractivo: My secret paradise.
  2. Introducción que enganche al lector: una pregunta (Have you ever…?) o una frase sorprendente.
  3. Desarrollo (1–2 párrafos): responde a las preguntas con ejemplos y detalles.
  4. Conclusión con tu opinión o una recomendación: So, if you ever need to relax, give it a try!
My secret paradise

Where do you go when life gets too busy? For me, the answer is easy: my grandparents' garden in a small village near Teruel.

There's an old fig tree in the corner, and I love lying in its shade with a good book. Sometimes I help my grandfather with the vegetables, or we just sit and chat while the birds sing. There's no Wi-Fi, which at first I hated, but now I think it's the best thing about the place.

Why do I like it so much? Because time seems to slow down there. I always come home calm and full of energy.

So, if you have a quiet place like this, don't forget to visit it often!
✍ Practica: "Articles wanted! What's the best present you have ever received? Who gave it to you, and why was it special?"

Parte 2 · Historia

Te dan la primera frase de la historia: "As soon as Lucy opened the door, she knew something was wrong." Debes continuarla (misma persona, mismo tiempo verbal) y darle un final.

Claves

  • Usa tiempos pasados variados: past simple, past continuous (was waiting), past perfect (had forgotten).
  • Conectores de tiempo: at first, then, a few minutes later, suddenly, while, as soon as, in the end.
  • Vocabulario de emociones y descripción: terrified, relieved, delighted, a strange noise, pitch dark.
  • Algo de diálogo da vida: "Who's there?" she whispered.
  • Un final claro (con sorpresa si es posible). No termines con «and then I woke up».
As soon as Lucy opened the door, she knew something was wrong. The living room was dark and the window was wide open, although she was sure she had closed it that morning.

Her heart was beating fast. Then she heard a noise coming from the kitchen. "Who's there?" she whispered, picking up an umbrella.

Slowly, she walked towards the kitchen and switched on the light. There, sitting on the table and eating the remains of her dinner, was an enormous grey cat!

Lucy started laughing with relief. The cat looked at her as if it lived there. In the end, she gave it some milk – and it has been living with her ever since.
✍ Practica: Your story must begin with this sentence: "I was walking home when my phone rang."
